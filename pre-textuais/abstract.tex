\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
    This work presents a comparative performance analysis of enterprise Wi-Fi networks (Ruckus and UniFi) using physical and logical telemetry. Based on an empirical dataset comprising 20 days with telemetry logs collected in a high-density university campus (spanning from 23/06/2026 to 04/09/2026), containing nearly 500,000 records (498,034 samples), the study proposes a structured pipeline for data cleaning, statistical hypothesis testing, and heuristic and unsupervised bottleneck classification. Mann-Whitney tests revealed significant disparities ($p < 0.001$) between the 2.4~GHz and 5~GHz bands, and discrepant retransmission rates between manufacturers (UniFi with 21.7\% vs. Ruckus with 5.6\% on average). Bottleneck classification indicated that the most prevalent limiting factors were weak physical link quality (G1) and roaming instability (G4), while physical cabling bottleneck (G5) was residual ($0.01\%$). The K-Means algorithm was employed to identify operational profiles of access points, whose clustering quality was evaluated by Silhouette (0.3383) and Davies-Bouldin (0.7953) metrics. This research contributes a reproducible methodology for characterizing conditions associated with potential bottlenecks in enterprise wireless networks.

   \noindent
   \textbf{Keywords}: Network Telemetry. Enterprise Wi-Fi. K-Means. Statistical Analysis. Bottleneck Diagnosis.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
