\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
    This work presents a comparative performance analysis of enterprise Wi-Fi networks (Ruckus and UniFi) using physical and logical telemetry. Based on an empirical dataset comprising effective telemetry monitoring collected in a high-density university campus (spanning from 23/06/2026 to 16/10/2026, totaling 48 to 49 monitored academic days), containing over 1.3 million records (1,349,925 clean samples: 1,202,408 from Ruckus and 147,517 from UniFi), the study proposes a structured pipeline for data cleaning, statistical hypothesis testing, and heuristic and unsupervised bottleneck classification. Mann-Whitney tests revealed significant disparities ($p < 0.001$) between the 2.4~GHz and 5~GHz bands, and discrepant retransmission rates between manufacturers (UniFi with 20.4\% vs. Ruckus with 5.7\% on average). Bottleneck classification with a calibrated mobility threshold ($> 15$ daily transitions) indicated that the most prevalent limiting factors were channel medium congestion (G2) and weak physical link quality (G1), while roaming instability (G4) affected 15.3\% of samples in Ruckus and 6.6\% in UniFi, and physical cabling bottleneck (G5) remained residual ($\le 0.01\%$). The K-Means algorithm was employed to identify operational profiles of access points, whose clustering quality was evaluated by Silhouette and Davies-Bouldin metrics. This research contributes a reproducible methodology for characterizing conditions associated with potential bottlenecks in enterprise wireless networks.

   \noindent
   \textbf{Keywords}: Network Telemetry. Enterprise Wi-Fi. K-Means. Statistical Analysis. Bottleneck Diagnosis.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
